Oldbury Court Estate, Bristol — eclipse sightline model

Partial solar eclipse, Wednesday 12 August 2026 · observer at 51.4881038°N, 2.5302209°W (Frome valley, 51 m) · terrain from EU-DEM 25 m · sun positions from NOAA solar-position algorithm

Verdict: yes on terrain — but this is a valley, and the margin is thinner

The sun clears the terrain skyline for the whole eclipse: +19.6° at first contact falling to +2.9° at fourth. So on bare ground alone, it works. But unlike a hilltop site, here the skyline is close — a suburban ridge 1.5–2.6 km west, standing 0.8° to 1.4° above your eye line rather than sitting at zero. You are looking up out of the Frome valley, not down over a city. Read the caveats before committing to this one.

Key moments

What the model is and isn't

  • Terrain source: EU-DEM v1.1, 25 m posts, queried via OpenTopoData. 39 radial sightlines from 248° to 305°, 130 range samples each out to 60 km (25 m spacing near-field, coarsening with distance), plus two grids for the 3D mesh.
  • Who's on the skyline: almost entirely the Purdown / Lockleaze ridge, 1.5–2.2 km west, topping out at 81–91 m. Along the sun's bearings that ridge stands 0.79–0.96° above your eye line, rising to 1.44° by 294°. Nothing beyond it is ever visible in this sector.
  • The ground profile WNW tells the story: 51 m where you stand, down to 36–45 m across the Frome floodplain for 1.4 km, then up to 84 m at 2 km. A bowl with a rim.
  • Geometry: apparent altitude = atan((htarget − heye − d²/2R') / d), with R' = R/(1−k), k = 0.13 — the standard refraction coefficient. Eye height 1.6 m.
  • Sun positions computed independently with the NOAA algorithm and cross-checked against timeanddate.com: agreement within 0.1° on all three contact points.
  • The DEM puts this spot at 51 m. Local relief within the 1.6 km patch runs from 33 m (the river) to 78 m, so small errors in where exactly you stand matter more here than on an open summit — move 100 m and the numbers shift.
  • Bare earth only. EU-DEM is a terrain model. It contains no trees and no buildings.

Trees. This is a wooded river valley.

EU-DEM is bare earth: no trees, no buildings. At an open hilltop that's a footnote. At Oldbury Court, where the Frome valley is lined with mature woodland, it is the question. Here is what it takes to block the sun at fourth contact (3.86°, bearing 289°), measured as height above local ground:

So anything within about 120 m to the west that is taller than a house will cost you the end of the eclipse. Beyond 150 m the ground drops away into the valley and you are safe from all but implausible timber.

What to do: pick an open meadow with at least 150 m of clear westward ground, and check the bearing band 268°–290° specifically — that is where the sun actually is, from first contact to last. Then add whatever sits on the Purdown ridge 2 km out: houses and mature trees there contribute roughly 0.3°, which eats about a tenth of your remaining margin. Survivable, but no longer the comfortable cushion a hilltop gives you.

Eyes

93.7% magnitude is not "nearly dark, therefore safe". At every moment of this eclipse there is un-eclipsed photosphere on view. Use ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses, or a pinhole/binocular projection — never look through unfiltered binoculars, a camera viewfinder or a telescope. A low sun feels comfortable to look at, which is exactly what makes it dangerous.
